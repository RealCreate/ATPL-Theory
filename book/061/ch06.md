## Chapter 6 · Time

Time and longitude are two ways of saying the same thing: the Earth turns 360° in 24 hours, so every 15° of longitude is an hour of time. This chapter covers local mean time and UTC, standard and daylight saving time, the International Date Line, and the times of sunrise, sunset and twilight that decide when a flight is in daylight or at night.

### 6.1 The mean solar day and local mean time

The **apparent solar day**, from one noon to the next, is not constant. It varies through the year because the Earth's orbit is elliptical and its axis tilted. The **mean solar day** is the average length, exactly **24 hours**, based on an imaginary *mean Sun* moving uniformly along the equator. **Local mean time** (LMT) is time kept by the mean Sun at a particular meridian: it is 12:00 LMT when the mean Sun crosses that meridian. Every meridian has its own LMT, and LMT at Greenwich is, for practical purposes, UTC. The day measured against the stars, the *sidereal day*, is about 23 h 56 min. `061.05.01.01.01`

### 6.2 Converting longitude and time

The Earth turns **360° in 24 h**, so **15° = 1 h**, **1° = 4 min** and **15' = 1 min**. This conversion is called *arc to time*. East of Greenwich the Sun crosses the meridian earlier, so local time is ahead of UTC:

**LMT = UTC + longitude east; LMT = UTC − longitude west**

The memory aid is "longitude east, Greenwich least; longitude west, Greenwich best". If the answer crosses midnight, the date changes too, so carry the day carefully. `061.05.01.02.01`

![Longitude is time: seen from above the North Pole, every 15° of longitude is one hour, with UTC at Greenwich and the date line opposite](img/gn-time.jpg)

> **Worked example · arc to time.** 0800 UTC at 045° E: 45° = 3 h, so LMT = 0800 + 3 h = **1100**. At 082° 30' W: 82° = 5 h 28 min and 30' = 2 min, so 5 h 30 min, and LMT = 0800 − 5 h 30 min = **0230**.

> **Key numbers · arc to time**
>
> | Arc | Time |
> | --- | --- |
> | 360° | 24 h |
> | 15° | 1 h |
> | 1° | 4 min |
> | 15' | 1 min |
> | 1' | 4 s |

### 6.3 Standard time and daylight saving time

**Standard time** is the legal time used throughout a country or zone. It is usually a whole number of hours (sometimes a half) from UTC, and it is set by each state, not fixed by longitude. **Daylight saving time**, or summer time, usually adds one hour to standard time in summer. The values for each country are listed in the AIP (GEN 2.1) and in almanacs. Mainland Spain, for example, keeps UTC + 1 in winter and UTC + 2 in summer. Flight plans, METARs and NOTAMs use UTC; local time appears mainly for opening hours, often marked "(summer)". To go between two local times, always convert to UTC first. `061.05.02.01.01`

### 6.4 The International Date Line

The **International Date Line** runs roughly along the 180° meridian, bending around islands and countries so that each keeps a single date. Crossing it **westbound**, from America towards Asia, **add a day**; crossing it **eastbound**, from Asia towards America, **subtract a day**. UTC itself does not change at the date line; only the local date does. The simplest way to avoid date-line mistakes is to work every timing problem in UTC. `061.05.02.02.01`

> **Exam trap.** Westbound across the date line the date goes forward one day; eastbound it goes back.

### 6.5 Sunrise, sunset and twilight

**Sunrise** and **sunset** are the moments when the **upper edge (limb)** of the Sun is on the visible horizon. At that moment the Sun's centre is geometrically about 50' below the horizon: 16' for the Sun's semi-diameter plus 34' for atmospheric refraction, which bends the light over the horizon.

**Civil twilight** is the period between sunset and the moment the Sun's centre is **6° below the horizon**, and likewise in the morning before sunrise. For aviation, night is commonly defined as the time from the end of evening civil twilight to the beginning of morning civil twilight. *Nautical* twilight (Sun 12° below) and *astronomical* twilight (18°) are not used for aviation night.

Almanacs tabulate these times in **LMT** for a given latitude and date. Convert to UTC with the longitude (arc to time), and then to standard time if needed. `061.05.03.01.01`

![Sunrise, sunset and twilight: the Sun's depression for civil, nautical and astronomical twilight, and why twilight is longer at high latitude](img/gn-twilight.jpg)

> **Worked example · sunset in UTC.** The almanac gives sunset 1842 LMT at a place at 012° W. 12° = 48 min, and the place is west, so UTC = 1842 + 48 min = **1930 UTC**.

Sunrise, sunset and twilight change with three things. `061.05.03.01.02`

- **Date.** Days lengthen in spring and summer and shorten in autumn and winter.
- **Latitude.** At the equator sunrise and sunset are near 0600 and 1800 LMT all year, and twilight is short because the Sun rises and sets steeply. At higher latitudes daylight varies much more through the year and twilight lasts longer, because the Sun crosses the horizon at a shallow angle; in high-latitude summer twilight can last all night.
- **Altitude.** From height you see further over the horizon, so sunrise is earlier and sunset later than on the ground. After sunset on the ground, an aircraft overhead can still be in sunlight.

The **length of daylight changes fastest around the equinoxes**, in March and September, when the Sun's declination changes most quickly, and most slowly around the solstices in June and December, when the declination is near its limit and almost still. For a few weeks around a solstice the day length hardly changes. `061.05.03.01.03`

### Check yourself

1. What is 1° of longitude in time, and 1' of longitude? *4 min; 4 s.*
2. It is 2200 UTC on 3 March. What is the LMT at 120° E? *0600 LMT on 4 March.*
3. It is 1530 LMT at 073° 15' W. What is UTC? *73° 15' = 4 h 53 min, so 2023 UTC.*
4. An aircraft crosses the date line flying from Tokyo to Los Angeles. What happens to the local date? *It goes back one day; UTC is unaffected.*
5. Where on the Earth is twilight shortest, and why? *At the equator, because the Sun rises and sets almost vertically.*
6. What depression of the Sun ends evening civil twilight? *6° below the horizon (centre of the Sun).*
7. When does the length of the day change fastest? *Around the equinoxes, in March and September.*
